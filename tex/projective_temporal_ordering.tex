  \begin{abstract}
    We identify a monotone proxy for temporal ordering within the
    Cosmochrony spectral admissibility programme.
    The natural observable along the BFS cascade is not the residual
    capacity $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$, which decays by construction,
    but the cumulative projected capacity
    $I(n) = \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(0) - \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$,
    which measures the spectral structure already projected at depth~$n$.
    For $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$, $I(n)$ is found to be strictly
    non-decreasing at every BFS step, for every conjugate pair
    $(c, q{-}c)$, with zero physically meaningful regressions observed
    across 274~pairs and 360~steps after burn-in.
    The temporal ordering is not a dynamics on the substrate~$\chi$, but
    an induced ordering relation on admissible projections, compatible
    with the observable rank stability established in O24~\cite{Beau2026a28}.
    The saturation plateau of $I(n)$ is interpreted as Born--Infeld
    saturation at the pair level.
  \end{abstract}

  \paragraph{Keywords.}
    non-injective projection; projective temporal ordering; spectral admissibility; cumulative projected
    capacity; one-way activation; Weil representation; Heisenberg group; Gram--Schmidt dynamics; emergent time

  \section{Introduction}

  The status of time in fundamental physics remains unsettled.
  In classical mechanics, time appears as an external parameter; in
  general relativity, it is incorporated into spacetime geometry; in
  quantum theory, it remains an external evolution parameter with no
  operator counterpart.
  Despite their empirical success, these frameworks do not provide a
  unified account of the origin of temporal ordering.

  In thermodynamic approaches, the arrow of time is associated with
  entropy increase.
  However, such accounts presuppose probabilistic structure and
  coarse-graining, and do not explain why a preferred ordering exists at
  the level of fundamental description.
  More recent approaches attempt to derive time from correlations,
  quantum entanglement, or relational observables, but typically retain
  an underlying notion of evolution or state update.

  The Cosmochrony framework adopts a different starting point~\cite{Beau2026a}.
  The relational substrate~$\chi$ is static, and no fundamental time
  parameter is assumed.
  Observable dynamics arises instead from an iterated reprojection
  sequence
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:reprojection}
    U_{n+1} = \Pi \circ \sigma(U_n),
  \end{equation}
  where $\Pi$ is a fixed non-injective projection and
  $\sigma(U_n) \in \Pi^{-1}(U_n)$ selects an admissible configuration
  within the fibre compatible with the current effective state.
  In this setting, the notion of time cannot be identified with an
  evolution of~$\chi$, but must emerge from the structure of admissible
  projections itself.

  This raises a precise question: does there exist a scalar observable,
  defined intrinsically along the admissibility cascade, that induces a
  consistent temporal ordering without introducing additional structure?

  The spectral admissibility programme provides a natural testing ground
  for this question.
  For each prime~$q$ and each conjugate pair $(c, q{-}c)$, the pipeline
  produces a sequence of observables $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$ indexed
  by BFS depth~$n$, interpreted as residual projective
  capacity~\cite{Beau2026a29}.
  While $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$ is not suitable as a temporal
  parameter due to its decreasing behaviour, its complement
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:I_intro}
    I(n) = \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(0) - \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)
  \end{equation}
  measures the cumulative projected capacity, i.e.\ the amount of
  spectral structure stably realised up to depth~$n$.

  The central result of this work is that $I(n)$ is strictly
  non-decreasing along all tested admissible trajectories, across all
  conjugate pairs and all primes $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$.
  This monotonicity is not imposed but observed numerically at each step
  of the cascade, with no physically meaningful regression.

  We interpret this property as defining a \emph{projective temporal
  ordering}: time is not a parameter of the substrate, but a monotone
  ordering induced by the accumulation of admissible spectral structure
  under a non-injective projection.
  This ordering is compatible with the stability of observable rank
  established in O24~\cite{Beau2026a28} and introduces no additional
  dynamical degrees of freedom.

  This perspective suggests that temporal ordering may arise as a
  structural consequence of non-injectivity, rather than as a fundamental
  ingredient of the theory.
  The cumulative observable $I(n)$ provides a concrete and computable
  realisation of this idea within the spectral admissibility cascade.

  \paragraph{Relation to measurement-induced arrows of time.}
    Recent work shows that the arrow of time in monitored quantum systems
    depends explicitly on the measurement protocol and can be statistically
    reversed by engineered Hamiltonians combined with feedback~\cite{garcia-pintos2026arrow}.
    Within the present framework, this behaviour admits a direct structural
    interpretation.

    The monotonicity of the cumulative projected capacity $I(n)$ reflects
    the information loss induced by the non-injective projection~$\Pi$.
    A feedback-controlled reversal does not contradict this structure:
    it compensates for this loss by injecting additional external information,
    effectively reconstructing part of the preimage of~$\Pi$.
    This supports the interpretation that temporal asymmetry is not a primitive
    feature of the substrate, but an emergent consequence of projective
    selection under admissibility constraints.

  \subsection{Conditional Quantum Models and the Limits of Reversible Emergence}

  Several approaches to the emergence of time in quantum theory rely on
  conditional structures rather than fundamental evolution.
  A notable example is the Page--Wootters construction, in which a global stationary state
  $|\Psi\rangle$ on a composite Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}_C \otimes \mathcal{H}_S$
  induces an apparent evolution via conditional states
  \[
    |\psi(t)\rangle := \langle t | \Psi \rangle.
  \]

  This construction can be interpreted as a family of conditional projections
  \[
    \Pi_t(\Psi) := \langle t | \Psi \rangle,
  \]
  which generate an ordering parameter $t$ without introducing an external time.

  From the present perspective, this mechanism shares a structural similarity
  with the projective cascade: both frameworks extract an effective sequence
  of states from a higher-level configuration via projection.

  However, a crucial difference arises.
  In conditional quantum models, the projection is not structurally non-injective in the physical sense: the
  global state $|\Psi\rangle$ is assumed to be fully recoverable, and the apparent evolution remains reversible.
  The ordering parameter $t$ labels different conditionings but does not define an intrinsically oriented sequence.

  By contrast, in the projective admissibility framework, the projection $\Pi$
  is structurally non-injective and induces irreversible information loss at
  each step.
  The cumulative projected capacity
  \[
    I(n) = \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(0) - \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)
  \]
  quantifies this loss as a monotone accumulation of realised structure.

  The observed strict monotonicity of $I(n)$ therefore provides a structural
  criterion distinguishing genuine temporal ordering from reversible
  conditional parametrisations: only when projection entails irreversible
  compression does an intrinsic arrow of time emerge.

  In this sense, conditional constructions such as Page--Wootters can be
  understood as reversible limits of the more general non-injective projection
  framework, in which ordering exists but lacks orientation.

  \paragraph{Relation to conditional quantum models.}
    Relational approaches to the emergence of time, such as the Page--Wootters
    construction~\cite{PageWootters1983}, recover an effective temporal ordering by
    conditioning on a clock subsystem within a globally stationary state.
    From the present perspective, such constructions involve a projection structure
    whose non-injective character is not exploited: the effective evolution is
    reconstructed without an associated cumulative loss of distinguishable information.
    The projective temporal ordering identified here differs in this respect,
    being tied to the monotone accumulation of projected capacity along the admissible
    cascade rather than to the choice of a conditioning sector.

  \section{The Two Variables and Why $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ Is Not the Right One}

  The observable $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) = \sigma_c(n)\,\sigma_{q-c}(n)$
  measures the residual projective capacity of the conjugate pair at
  depth~$n$: it counts, normalised by~$q$, the fraction of Weil
  fingerprint vectors that remain linearly independent of the
  Gram--Schmidt span built over the preceding shells.
  By construction, $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$ decays as~$n$ grows and
  cannot serve as a temporal proxy, since a decreasing quantity does not
  define an ordering compatible with the accumulation of structure.
  The correct variable is its complement~\eqref{eq:I_intro}, which we
  now define formally.

  \begin{definition}[Cumulative projected capacity]
Let $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$ denote the canonical pair
observable~\cite{Beau2026a29} at BFS depth~$n$.
The \emph{cumulative projected capacity} at depth~$n$ is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:I}
  I(n) \;:=\; \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(0) - \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n).
\end{equation}
  \end{definition}

  $I(n)$ measures the spectral structure stably projected up to depth~$n$:
  it starts at zero and accumulates as the cascade progresses, with
  $I(0) = 0$ and $I(n) \to \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(0)$ as
  $n \to n_{\mathrm{sat}}$, where $n_{\mathrm{sat}}$ denotes the
  saturation depth at which $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) \approx 0$.

  \begin{remark}
    $I(n)$ is non-decreasing if and only if $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$ is
    non-increasing.
    While this is expected under a dissipative interpretation of the BFS
    cascade, the present work establishes it empirically without relying on
    a formal dissipation theorem: the monotonicity of $I(n)$ is observed
    directly, step by step and pair by pair, across all tested primes.
  \end{remark}

  \paragraph{Independence from the BFS parametrisation.}
    Although $I(n)$ is indexed by BFS depth, its definition does not depend
    on the combinatorial parametrisation of the cascade but on the
    cumulative spectral projection.
    The monotonicity observed here is therefore not an artefact of BFS
    ordering, but reflects an intrinsic property of admissible projection:
    the Gram--Schmidt span can only grow within the canonical
    Gram--Schmidt construction used in the admissibility pipeline.

  \section{Temporal Ordering as a Projective Relation}

  \paragraph{Order versus dynamics.}
    The temporal structure proposed here is not a dynamics on the
    substrate~$\Omega$ and not an evolution of~$\chi$.
    It is an induced ordering relation on the set of admissible projectable
    configurations.
    The effective time parameter arises as a monotone ordering on admissible
    projections, defined by the progressive saturation of the Born--Infeld
    constraint under~$\Pi$.
    Time is therefore neither a parameter of~$\chi$ nor an independent
    structure on the observable space~$\mathcal{O}$, but a relational
    ordering induced by the projective admissibility structure.

  \paragraph{Temporal ordering and non-injectivity.}
    Because $\Pi$ is non-injective~\cite{Beau2026l}, a given observable
    state $o \in \mathcal{O}$ corresponds to a fibre $\Pi^{-1}(o)$
    containing multiple underlying configurations.
    Temporal ordering does not correspond to motion within~$\Omega$, but to
    a change in the admissible selection
    $\sigma(U_n) \in \Pi^{-1}(U_n)$ that defines the next effective state
    $U_{n+1} = \Pi \circ \sigma(U_n)$.
    Successive steps correspond to distinct admissible realisations drawn
    from different regions of the fibres.
    Time is therefore an ordering of admissible selections within a
    non-injective structure, rather than an evolution of the underlying
    configuration~$\chi$.

    Conversely, if $\Pi$ were injective, each observable would correspond
    to a unique underlying configuration and no ordering ambiguity would
    arise.
    The existence of temporal ordering is thus a direct consequence of
    non-injectivity: it reflects the structural impossibility of identifying
    a unique underlying configuration for each observable state.

    \begin{proposition}[Projective temporal ordering --- numerical form]
      \label{prop:order}
      Define an ordering on effective states along the BFS cascade by
      \begin{equation}
        \label{eq:order}
        U_n \prec U_{n+1}
        \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
        I(U_n) \leq I(U_{n+1}),
      \end{equation}
      where $n$ is the BFS shell depth, an ordering parameter on projected
      configurations that does not represent a physical time variable at the
      substrate level.
      For $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$, this ordering is strictly observed
      numerically along the tested BFS trajectories: $I(n)$ is strictly
      non-decreasing at every step, for every conjugate pair $(c, q{-}c)$,
      with no physically meaningful exception.
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{remark}[Status]
        This is numerical evidence, not a proof.
        The result establishes the ordering empirically along the tested
        trajectories and does not exclude the possibility of exceptions at
        larger~$q$ or along non-BFS paths.
        The one-way activation property---that modes, once stably projected,
        do not deactivate---remains to be established analytically.
    \end{remark}

    \begin{remark}[State machine structure of the admissible cascade]
      \label{rem:state-machine-pto}
      The admissible neighbourhood $\mathcal{A}(U_n)$ gives the projective
      cascade the structure of an oriented state machine: nodes are admissible
      projections, edges are admissible transitions, and a physical history
      is a directed path.
      When $|\mathcal{A}(U_n)| = 1$, the successor is uniquely determined by
      the admissibility structure — no external dynamical law is required.
      When branching occurs ($|\mathcal{A}(U_n)| > 1$), the relative
      admissibility weights of the branches play the role of quantum
      probabilities.
      In both cases, the index $n$ acquires its temporal meaning entirely
      from the orientation of the admissibility graph; $I(n)$ measures
      cumulative path length in the spectral sense, not elapsed time with
      respect to any pre-existing background.
      The dynamics of the framework is the oriented topology of this graph,
      not a law superimposed upon it.

      Moreover, the first transition $U_0 \to U_1$ requires no external
      trigger.
      The projective incompleteness result of~\cite{Beau2026a} establishes
      that any admissible initial projection $\Pi_0$ necessarily leaves a
      non-trivial residue, because a projection with trivial kernel would
      require the simultaneous resolution of non-commuting generators ---
      which is structurally forbidden by the Heisenberg structure of the
      admissible fibre.
      The admissible neighbourhood $\mathcal{A}(U_0)$ is therefore
      guaranteed to be non-empty: the ordering starts not as a postulate
      but as a structural necessity.
    \end{remark}

  \paragraph{Compatibility with observable rank stability.}
    The temporal ordering introduced here does not enlarge the observable
    rank.
    It acts entirely at the level of admissible realisations within the
    fixed observable rank established in O23~\cite{Beau2026a27} and
    stabilised in O24~\cite{Beau2026a28}.
    In particular, temporal progression does not enlarge
    $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathrm{Im}\,\Pi \cap \mathrm{Ntrl})$, but only
    reorders admissible configurations within this fixed structure.
    The ordering is therefore compatible with the vertical non-injectivity
    theorem and introduces no hidden dynamics.

  \section{Interpretation}

  \paragraph{One-way activation.}
    The zero-regression result means the admissible BFS cascade behaves as
    a one-way activation process in spectral space.
    Once a mode contributes stably to the Gram--Schmidt span, it does not
    leave: the spectral structure accumulated at depth~$n$ is preserved at
    all subsequent depths.
    One-way activation is a property of \emph{spectral modes}, not of the
    coherence of projected configurations: a coherent combination of modes
    --- corresponding to an unstable particle --- can lose its admissible
    coherence and reorganize into simpler configurations without any mode
    leaving~$V_n$.
    This one-way activation mechanism provides a structural form of
    irreversibility that does not rely on probabilistic or thermodynamic
    arguments, but follows from the admissible accumulation of projected
    spectral structure.
    It is the condition of possibility for the identification of $I(n)$ as
    a temporal proxy: an ordering defined by~$I$ is well-posed only if $I$
    never reverses.

  \paragraph{Non-arbitrariness of $I(n)$.}
    The choice of $I(n)$ as a temporal proxy is not arbitrary among monotone
    functions: it is uniquely determined within the present framework as the
    cumulative quantity associated with admissible spectral projection, and
    therefore carries direct physical meaning.
    It is bounded above by $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(0)$ (the Born--Infeld
    capacity of the pair) and its increments are exactly the newly projected
    spectral structure at each BFS step.
    The cumulative observable $I(n)$ is moreover expected to reflect the
    scaling properties encoded in the capacity exponent
    $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$~\cite{Beau2026a29}, since both quantify the
    rate at which admissible spectral structure is resolved along the
    cascade; in particular, a larger value of $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ is
    expected to correspond to a faster accumulation of $I(n)$.
    Establishing this relation quantitatively remains an open problem
    (Question~3 below).

  \paragraph{Information-theoretic interpretation.}
    The cumulative projected capacity $I(n)$ admits a natural interpretation in
    information-theoretic terms.
    Since $\Pi$ is non-injective~\cite{Beau2026l}, distinct admissible configurations
    at depth $n-1$ may map to the same observable state at depth $n$, implying a
    reduction of distinguishable information at the observable level.
    The quantity $I(n)$ measures the spectral structure stably projected up to depth $n$
    and is therefore compatible with an interpretation as a cumulative proxy for the
    information loss induced by $\Pi$.
    No strict identification with a projection entropy is assumed here;
    the observed monotonicity of $I(n)$ nonetheless supports the view that the
    projective temporal ordering reflects the progressive reduction of distinguishable
    information along the admissible cascade.

  \paragraph{Saturation plateau and Born--Infeld bound.}
    $I(n)$ reaches its maximum $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(0)$ when the
    residual capacity is exhausted.
    This plateau is interpreted as Born--Infeld saturation at the pair
    level: once the projective flux budget of the conjugate pair is fully
    consumed, no additional spectral structure can be resolved within the
    effective description.
    Saturation is irreversible in all tested cases; this is consistent with
    the bounded-flux constraint of the Cosmochrony
    framework~\cite{Beau2026a} but is not derived from it here.

  \paragraph{Candidate arrow of time.}
    The strict monotonicity of $I(n)$ provides a candidate ordering
    compatible with an arrow of time, without invoking thermodynamic entropy
    or a background temporal coordinate.
    The ordering is projective: it reflects the irreversible accumulation
    of stably activated spectral structure along the admissible cascade.
    This provides a candidate identification of the temporal direction with the
    direction of increasing information loss induced by the non-injective projection~$\Pi$,
    as quantified by the monotone accumulation of $I(n)$.
    Whether this identification holds within the full
    framework~\cite{Beau2026a} is left as an open question; the present
    result establishes only its monotone and stable character along the
    tested BFS trajectories for $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$.

  \paragraph{Relation to standard time-dependent evolution.}
    The present construction does not assume a fundamental time parameter.
    Temporal ordering is instead defined by the monotone accumulation of
    projected spectral capacity along the admissible cascade.
    In this reading, time-dependent evolution equations such as the
    Schrödinger equation~\cite{Schrodinger1926} are naturally understood
    as effective descriptions formulated within a pre-existing temporal
    ordering, rather than as fundamental dynamical laws from which time
    itself originates.
    This perspective connects with longstanding discussions of the problem
    of time in quantum gravity~\cite{Isham1993,Anderson2012}, where the
    absence of an external time parameter in the Wheeler--DeWitt
    equation~\cite{Wheeler1967} motivates relational and emergent
    approaches to temporal
    ordering~\cite{PageWootters1983,Rovelli1991,Connes1994}.
    This perspective also suggests a parallel with the distinction between
    time-dependent and time-independent formulations in quantum theory:
    while the time-dependent Schrödinger equation describes evolution with
    respect to an external parameter, the present construction is closer in
    spirit to a spectral characterization, in which the relevant structure
    is encoded in the set of admissible modes rather than in a fundamental
    time evolution.
    Whether the projective ordering identified here is compatible with such
    effective descriptions in appropriate limits remains an open question.

  \paragraph{Temporal ordering without a common manifold.}
    This projective notion of temporal ordering is also what one expects if space-time is not a single
    arena shared by all observers.
    Recent no-go results show that rejecting the absoluteness of observed events is incompatible with
    embedding all events in one common background space-time, forcing space-time notions to be indexed to
    each observer~\cite{Pienaar2026}.
    Unlike approaches that replace a single space-time by observer-indexed space-time manifolds, the
    present construction reconstructs temporal order directly from admissibility stratification through
    $I(n)$, without introducing a manifold $M_A$ for each observer.
    If no global manifold is available to carry a preferred ordering, temporal succession cannot be read
    off a background geometry and must instead be reconstructed intrinsically from the admissibility
    cascade; the cumulative capacity $I(n)$ supplies, within a single projectable trajectory, the ordering
    that a fragmented space-time can no longer provide globally.

  \subsection*{Formal statement and what remains to be proved}

  The numerical results above support the following conjecture.

  \begin{conjecture}[One-way activation]
    \label{conj:owa}
    For all admissible primes~$q$, all conjugate pairs $(c, q{-}c)$, and
    all $n \geq 1$:
    \begin{equation}
      \label{eq:owa}
      \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) \;\leq\; \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n-1).
    \end{equation}
  \end{conjecture}

  Three structural facts are immediate from the Gram--Schmidt construction.
  Let $V_n = \mathrm{span}\{f_\omega : \omega \in S_0 \cup \cdots \cup S_n\}$
  denote the cumulative span of Weil fingerprint vectors up to shell~$n$.
  Then:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $V_n \subseteq V_{n+1}$ (the span is non-decreasing),
    \item $\dim V_n \leq q$ (the ambient space has finite dimension~$q$),
    \item $\delta r_n = \dim V_n - \dim V_{n-1} \geq 0$ (the rank gain is
    non-negative).
  \end{itemize}
  These facts imply that the cumulative rank $\dim V_n$ is
  non-decreasing and that $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n) \to 0$
  asymptotically as $\dim V_n \to q$.
  They do not, however, imply the stepwise monotonicity of
  $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$, which is the content of
  Conjecture~\ref{conj:owa}.
  However, they do not imply Conjecture~\ref{conj:owa}: cumulative rank
  monotonicity alone does not control the shellwise ratio
  \begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ratio}
    \sigma_c(n) \;=\; \frac{\delta r_n}{|S_n|},
  \end{equation}
  since both numerator and denominator may grow with~$n$.
  The conjecture requires, in addition, control of the efficiency with
  which successive BFS shells generate new independent Weil fingerprints
  relative to shell size.

  The numerical results of the present paper show that this efficiency
  decreases monotonically throughout the tested range.
  A proof would require quantitative control of shellwise spectral
  saturation in the Weil sector of $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$,
  which we leave as an open problem (Question~2 in Section~\ref{sec:open}).

  \section{Open Questions}
  \label{sec:open}

  \begin{enumerate}

    \item Does strict monotonicity of $I(n)$ hold for all admissible
    primes~$q$, or are there exceptions at large~$q$?
    The extension to $q = 211$ (105 pairs, 50 steps) confirms strict
    monotonicity with zero regressions (Table~\ref{tab:stability}).
    Extension to $q \in \{307, 401\}$ is pending.

    \item Can the one-way activation property---$\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$
    non-increasing along the full cascade---be proved analytically from
    the structure of the BFS expansion and the admissibility constraints
    of~$\chi$?

    \item What is the precise relationship between $I(n)$ and the capacity
    exponent $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$~\cite{Beau2026a29}?
    Is $I(n)$ sensitive to the value of $\delta_{\mathrm{pair}}$ or only
    to its sign?

    \item Does the saturation depth (the~$n$ at which $I(n)$ plateaus)
    carry physical meaning independent of~$q$?

    \item What is the precise relationship between BFS shell depth and
    effective resolution-cell entry?
    Preliminary numerical evidence suggests that the characteristic
    shell~$n^*$ at which $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$ first undergoes a
    qualitative knee increases with~$q$ over the tested cases
    $q \in \{29, 61, 101\}$, with approximate values
    $n^*(29) \approx 4$, $n^*(61) \approx 6$, $n^*(101) \approx 8$.
    This suggests that BFS shells do not define resolution cells, but
    may reveal the depth at which such cells become effective.
    The structural status, scaling law, and analytical derivation of
    this relation are left open.

    \item Does the characteristic shell $n^*(q)$ of Question~5 coincide
    with, or bound, the \emph{locking depth} $n_c(q)$ at which the BFS
    admissibility structure transitions from a locally dominated regime to
    a universally dominated one?
    The companion paper Q10~\cite{Beau2026q10} establishes that the
    horizontal metric coefficient $A_H(q)$ satisfies $A_H(q) \to 2$ as
    $q \to \infty$, where $2 = C_{\mathrm{su}(2)}|_{j=1}$ is the
    Casimir eigenvalue on $\mathrm{Sym}^2(V_\rho)$.
    At $q = 211$, the anomalous value $A_H(211) = 2.022$ (against the
    monotone trend at $q \leq 151$) is attributed in Q10 to insufficient
    BFS coverage at $\mathrm{bfs\_frac} = 0.11$, consistent with
    $n_c(211)$ exceeding the tested window at that fraction.
    This suggests that $n_c(q)$ is precisely the depth below which
    finite-coverage artefacts prevent the su(2)-isotropic structure
    from being resolved — analogous to the knee $n^*(q)$ at which
    $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}$ changes regime.
    Whether $n^*(q) \sim n_c(q)$, and whether $n_c(q)$ can be derived
    analytically from the admissibility structure of
    $\mathrm{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$, are open questions whose
    resolution would connect the spectral ordering of the present paper
    to the effective geometry results of~\cite{Beau2026q10}.

  \end{enumerate}

  \section{Conclusion}

  The observable $\sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$ measures residual projective
  capacity and decays along the BFS cascade; its complement
  $I(n) = \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(0) - \sigma_{\mathrm{pair}}(n)$
  measures cumulative projected capacity and grows.
  Across $q \in \{29, 61, 101, 151, 211\}$, covering 274~pairs and
  360~post-burn-in steps, $I(n)$ is strictly non-decreasing at every BFS
  step and for every conjugate pair, with no physically meaningful
  regression.
  This establishes $I(n)$ as a robust monotone proxy for temporal ordering
  along the admissible spectral cascade.
  The ordering is not a dynamics on~$\chi$ but a relational structure
  induced by the non-injective projection~$\Pi$: it reorders admissible
  fibre selections within the fixed observable rank of O24~\cite{Beau2026a28},
  without introducing additional observable degrees of freedom.
  The one-way activation property observed here is a numerical result;
  its analytical derivation from the admissibility constraints of~$\Pi$
  and the scaling of $n_1(q)/q$~\cite{Beau2026a29} remains the central
  open direction.
  The monotonic accumulation of $I(n)$ is moreover compatible with an
  interpretation as cumulative information loss under non-injective projection,
  identifying the one-way activation property as the spectral signature of
  structural irreversibility.
  These results support the view that temporal ordering is an emergent consequence
  of admissible projection structure, rather than a primitive ingredient of physical
  theory.
